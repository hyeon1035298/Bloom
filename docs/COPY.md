# Bloom copy

Bloom's copy follows [Writing: Product Copy](https://github.com/115dkk/make-interfaces-feel-better), licensed under MIT. The guide includes material adapted from [jakubkrehel/skills](https://github.com/jakubkrehel/skills), also MIT.

- Name the effect first. Explain implementation only when it helps a choice.
- Use labels, instructions, facts and results. Avoid greetings, praise, slogans, personal pronouns in labels and exclamation marks.
- Errors name the failure and the next action. Keep exceptions, command output and diagnostic codes in logs or details.
- Put the action beside the problem, or link directly to its control. Keep help short and conditional.
- Use one term per concept. Preserve product names, key names and search aliases. Localize whole templates, including variable text.
- Shorten text to fit; retain font sizes. Verify every locale and interpolation hole.

## Registers

Korean sentences use 합니다체. Status, empty states, short results and labels use noun phrases; buttons and menus have no sentence ending. Instructions end in -세요. Do not use 해요체 statements, 내 in labels, or -아/어 주다, -드리다 or -두다 for Bloom's actions. Requirements say what the reader must, may or cannot do. Korean documentation uses 합니다체 throughout.

English uses sentence case, direct verbs and short factual statements. Status and labels are noun phrases. No persona, “we,” “my” labels or exclamation marks.

Japanese sentences use です/ます; status and labels use 体言止め, and buttons use plain action names. Simplified Chinese is concise and direct, without 您, 吧, 哦, 啦 or exclamation marks. Product names and keyboard tokens remain unchanged. Search examples retain accepted Korean/English input because Bloom's parsers support those languages.

Keep prompts sent to AI unchanged. Apply this voice to authored demo responses and all interface text; user content and live AI responses retain their authors' wording.
